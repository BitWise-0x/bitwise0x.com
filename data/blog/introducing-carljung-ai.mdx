Most AI reflection and journaling tools are text conversations. You type, the model replies, and what remains is a transcript. What I found missing is everything a transcript discards. Jung argued that the psyche reveals itself less in what we declare than in what escapes around it, for example the pause before an answer or the joke that deflects a question a little too well. None of that survives in text.

I built [CarlJung.ai](https://carljung.ai) to explore whether voice could do better. It is a self-reflection service in which you speak with a Carl Jung persona. It listens to how you say things as well as what you say, and it uses Jung's own framework of individuation to give the sessions a direction.

The service consists of four parts:

1. An archetype test
2. Voice sessions with a persona that knows your result
3. Written notes after each session
4. Progression through the four stages of individuation

> [!IMPORTANT]
> [CarlJung.ai](https://carljung.ai) is a voice journaling tool for adults. It is not a substitute for licensed mental-health care, therapy, or medical advice.

## Why Jung

---

Most AI companions amount to a system prompt and an endless supply of warmth. They agree with whatever you bring them, which is pleasant but does not lead anywhere. I wanted something with structure, and Jung's psychology provides one. Individuation is his term for the lifelong work of integrating the parts of yourself that you have split off, and it can be described in four stages:

- **Persona** - recognizing the social mask you assemble for other people
- **Shadow** - naming the qualities you have disowned
- **Anima / Animus** - the inner figure that shapes how you relate to others
- **Self** - the direction the other three point toward

A staged model lends itself to a service, because it has a starting point and a way to tell whether a person is progressing or returning to the same ground. Jung did not publish these as a numbered ladder, so they are better treated as a map than as a curriculum.

## The Archetype Test

---

The starting point is a test of twelve questions based on Carol Pearson's twelve archetypes. For each question you choose the answer closest to you and also the one furthest from you. The second choice is significant. In an ordinary questionnaire, what a person rejects has to be inferred from whatever scores lowest. Here it is stated directly.

<ZoomableImage
  src="/static/images/blog/carljung/assessment.png"
  alt="CarlJung.ai archetype assessment"
  width={800}
  height={500}
/>

The result is a blend rather than a label. It consists of a dominant archetype, a secondary and a tertiary one beneath it, and a shadow, which is the archetype you rejected most often. For example, a profile might come back as mostly Sage with Creator and Hero close behind, and Caregiver as the shadow because it was marked furthest away more often than any other.

The test is free and does not require an account.

## The Voice Sessions

---

The sessions themselves are spoken. Each one is a live voice conversation with the Jung persona, which already knows your archetype blend, your current stage, and what emerged in the previous session.

<ZoomableImage
  src="/static/images/blog/carljung/chat-session.png"
  alt="CarlJung.ai voice session"
  width={1280}
  height={900}
/>

While you speak, the service reads emotion from your voice - the tone, the pitch, the rhythm and the pauses. It is worth being precise about what this means. It measures the sound of your voice rather than the meaning of your words. As a result, the persona has two sources to work from, what you said and how you said it.

## The Session Notes

---

Any session longer than a couple of minutes produces written notes. They cover the themes that emerged and your level of engagement, and on the top plan they extend to resistance and where your shadow appeared.

<ZoomableImage
  src="/static/images/blog/carljung/analysis.png"
  alt="CarlJung.ai session analysis"
  width={800}
  height={644}
/>

The most valuable notes come from the moments where the two sources disagree. Imagine saying "I don't feel anything about that" while your voice indicates embarrassment. A transcript would record that nothing was felt. The notes record the resistance instead.

The notes also feed a readiness score for your current stage. Once the score is high enough you are offered the next stage, and you decide when to take it. Progress therefore depends on how you engage rather than on the number of sessions you complete.

## A Persona, Not Jung

---

<Image
  src="/static/images/blog/carljung/code-profile.jpg"
  alt="Binary code projected on a face in profile"
  width={1200}
  height={800}
/>

Persona is Jung's own word. He took it from the Latin for an actor's mask and used it for the face a person builds to meet the world. What speaks in these sessions is a persona in exactly that sense. It is a constructed voice that knows his framework, with none of the man behind it, and I do not wish to suggest otherwise.

<Image
  src="/static/images/blog/carljung/jung-portrait.jpg"
  alt="Carl Gustav Jung, portrait photograph, ETH-Bibliothek Zurich"
  width={624}
  height={770}
/>

Nevertheless, Jung would probably have recognized the practice. He spent years in deliberate dialogue with inner figures, a method he called active imagination, and he treated what they said as information. A session here is a similar exercise. The difference is where the figure comes from. His arose from his own unconscious, whereas this one is assembled from his writing. In my view that makes it a mirror with a method rather than an analyst. The only unconscious in the conversation is yours.

## Conclusion

---

Jung wrote that the meeting of two personalities is like the contact of two chemical substances, and that if there is any reaction, both are transformed. A voice persona does not reproduce that, and I do not claim that it does. What I am testing is narrower. Does a structured framework, carried by a voice that can hear what lies beneath the words, give self-reflection more traction than a blank page or an open-ended chat? I suspect it does, but that will be answered over real sessions rather than by the design.
