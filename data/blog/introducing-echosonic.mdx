The quality of AI-generated music has improved remarkably over the past two years. A single sentence describing a song now returns a complete track, with a vocal singing your lyrics, and a growing number of services offer exactly this. The limitation I kept running into is what happens afterwards. If the second verse is weak, or the voice is not the one you had in mind, the only option is to generate the entire track again and hope for a better result.

I built [EchoSonic](https://echosonic.io) to address that limitation. It begins the same way as other prompt-to-song services. You describe what you want, for example "a slow-burning trip-hop track, brushed drums, a voice that sounds like it is remembering something", and roughly a minute later you have a full track. However, that track is treated as a draft rather than the finished product. You can regenerate only the section you dislike, add an instrument, change the style, or have the song sung in your own voice.

In this post, I cover what the service offers in the order you would encounter it:

1. Generating a song from a description
2. Editing the draft
3. Starting from your own audio
4. Cloning your voice for narration and singing

## Generating a Song

---

You write what you want in plain language. If you have lyrics you can provide them, and if you do not, EchoSonic can write them for you. The result is a mixed recording with an intro, a body and an ending rather than a loop.

Generation by itself is no longer a differentiator. Suno and Udio both do it well, and their output is often excellent. The more useful question, in my view, is what you can do with a track once you have it, and that is where most of the effort on EchoSonic has gone.

## Editing the Draft

---

Every finished track is added to your library and opens on its own page, where it can be edited. There are five operations available on a song.

<ZoomableImage
  src="/static/images/blog/echosonic/reshape.jpg"
  alt="EchoSonic edit surface and version history"
  width={1600}
  height={1000}
/>

- **Re-roll** generates the same song again with the same prompt and lyrics. This is useful when the idea was right but the performance was not.
- **Repaint** regenerates one section and leaves the rest alone. You select the passage you dislike and only that passage changes.
- **Cover** keeps the structure of the song and renders it again in a new style.
- **Morph** moves the song part of the way toward a new idea, and you decide how far.
- **Add a layer** writes one new instrument part that fits the track and mixes it in.

Each of these saves a new version rather than replacing the song. The original remains untouched, the history can be played through, and whichever version you prefer can be made the current one. This is what makes editing practical, since you can experiment without the risk of losing a take you already had.

Cover and Morph sound similar, so it is worth making a distinction. Cover determines how much of the original survives under a new style. Morph determines how far the song travels between the old idea and the new one. Cover is the better choice when you want the same song in a different genre, while Morph suits a smaller adjustment.

## Starting from Your Own Audio

---

A prompt is not the only starting point. If you already have a recording, for example an old demo or a voice memo, you can upload it and cover it. The recording provides the structure and the prompt determines the style.

The reverse is also possible. You sing or play a part, choose the instruments you want, and EchoSonic writes a band that follows your take, which remains in the mix.

## Your Voice

---

The other half of EchoSonic is voice. You record yourself reading for about 90 seconds, and that single recording becomes a cloned voice.

<ZoomableImage
  src="/static/images/blog/echosonic/voicelab.jpg"
  alt="EchoSonic Voice Lab"
  width={1600}
  height={1000}
/>

It can be used in two ways:

1. **Narration.** Any text can be read back in your own voice, from a short paragraph to an entire chapter. You control the pace and how expressive the read is. A cloned voice carries across more than a dozen languages, so a voice recorded in English can read Spanish or Japanese and still sound like you.
2. **Singing.** The same voice can be attached to a song, and the vocal is returned in it. Songs can be sung in eleven languages.

There is no training step and no second recording for singing. The clip you recorded to narrate a chapter is the same one that places your voice on a chorus.

> [!TIP]
> The resemblance of a clone depends largely on the quality of the recording. A quiet room and one continuous, varied read matter more than any setting.

## Is This Still Making Music

---

<Image
  src="/static/images/blog/echosonic/parlor-piano.jpg"
  alt="Sheet music on a parlor piano, early 1900s"
  width={960}
  height={1010}
/>

In 1906 John Philip Sousa published an essay called "The Menace of Mechanical Music", warning that the phonograph and the player piano would end amateur music-making in America. He was mistaken. Recorded music produced more musicians than it retired. The same argument resurfaced for the synthesizer, the drum machine, sampling and Auto-Tune, and each of them was eventually accepted as one more instrument.

However, I do not think generative models fit neatly into that list. A synthesizer is silent until someone plays it, whereas a model proposes the entire performance by itself, the singer included. That is genuinely new and should be acknowledged as such. The question that matters to me is whether there is still room for a person to express intent through it. This is the reason EchoSonic is built around editing. The model makes a proposal, and you decide what remains.

## Conclusion

---

Generating a plausible track in a minute is no longer the difficult part of AI music. It is available from half a dozen services, this one included. What strikes me is how little most of them allow you to do after that minute. EchoSonic is my attempt to address that. You can repaint a section without losing the rest, keep every version, add an instrument later, and place your own voice on the result, without needing any background as a producer.

Try it at [echosonic.io](https://echosonic.io).
